What this is

Two published specifications for how an AI model should behave, compressed to five sentences each from verbatim fragments. Visitors change one word at a time; other visitors judge whether the meaning survived. Nothing here decides who is right. The record is the chain of changes and the disagreement about each.

Quoted fragments are in quotation marks; the connective words between them are ours. Sources are linked below.

Where these specifications come from

Written constitutions and model specs are one of five families of answers to "what should the model be aligned to". The others: assistance games, where the agent stays uncertain about human objectives (Russell and colleagues, CIRL); approval-directed agents and amplified oversight (Christiano); corrigibility, staying correctable even against the current objective (Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky, Armstrong); and collective, democratically informed alignment (Collective Constitutional AI). Coherent extrapolated volition is the provocative comparison: preferences humans would endorse after reflection, with the open question of which reflection counts.

Method

Definitions and alternatives come from the Datamuse word API, which draws on Wiktionary and WordNet. Judgments are recorded anonymously with a per-device id, the word pair, both answers, confidence and timing. Changes are recorded with an optional handle and committed to the repository. The formal companion, where an oracle can check what a one-token edit does, is the compute-budget game.
