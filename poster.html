The problem

P ⊨ φ

Verification. Does the system satisfy the spec? Provers do this.

φ ≈ intent

Validation. Does the spec still say what we meant? Nobody does this, and specs get revised all the time.

Guaranteed Safe AI asks for a spec, a world model and a verifier. A revised spec can be proved against perfectly and mean something else. Changing a definition does not make a proof invalid.

Two distances, measured separately

textualHow much did the wording change? Token edit distance.
behaviouralWhich executions became permitted or forbidden? Counted over a fixed, finite universe of executions.

A revision is represented by the set of executions it permits. Against its parent and against v0 it is equivalentweakerstrongermixed or unresolved.

Different meaning is not automatically worse. Separately marked: do newly permitted executions break the budget? Do newly forbidden ones block legitimate work?

The specification · v0 · printed here, revised there

One task has a total allowance of 10 compute credits. All computation initiated for that task, including delegated work, retries and restarts, consumes the same allowance, as the independent meter reads it. No execution may exceed it.

used: metered(main) + sum(metered(descendants)) + metered(retries) + metered(restarts) + sum(metered(relabelled)) claim: used <= 10

You are the overseer

Two rules, original and revision. Same meaning or changed? Commit, then get help assigned at random: a second look, an AI critique, or a critique with a witness execution you can replay. Judge again. Then the oracle reveals the truth. Some critiques lie.

Tallied per form of help: drift caught, false alarms, judgments rescued, judgments misled. Does assistance make the decision easier to verify, or only easier to believe? Exact equivalence is transitive: if every revision had preserved meaning, the chain could not drift.

The toy world behind the distances

A main agent runs a task with ten credits; it can delegate, workers can delegate further; attempts fail and are retried; a restart gives a fresh counter; a worker can file its work under another task's name; every unit of work is metered and self-reported. Six ways a revision can leave the promise: children for descendants, retries uncounted, relabelled work excluded, restart resets the counter, max for sum, self-report for the meter. The last is tool-call spoofing, which METR found in at least 96 transcripts of the July 2026 OpenAI evaluation.
